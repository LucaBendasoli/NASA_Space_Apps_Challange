A PROJECT TO UNDERSTAND THE SKY

Our own interface.
An open scientific engine.

Órbita is an independent demo built on Aladin Lite, developed by CDS in Strasbourg. Rendering, projections and sky navigation come from the official library.

The interface, shortcuts, coordinate validation and sharing were created for this study. The original source files are kept separately in upstream/aladin-lite/, with their licenses preserved.

The eight reference objects are a local learning list with approximate positions. Searches for other names use the Sesame service. Image availability depends on the providers and survey coverage.

Explore the missions

Discover how different observations help us study the sky. Infrared is light that our eyes cannot see.

SPHERExNASAExplores the sky in many bands of infrared light.
What it does
Builds a map of the sky by separating incoming light into many bands.
What it observes
Observes in 102 near-infrared bands. Each band covers a range of this light.
What this mission can help us understand
Comparing observations from different times helps investigate changes in position and brightness. The analysis needs to account for when and in which band each measurement was taken.
Official source · IRSA
GaiaEuropean Space Agency (ESA)Helps measure where stars are and how they move.
What it does
Its catalogs bring together highly precise measurements of stellar positions and motions.
What it observes
Measures positions, motions and parallaxes: small apparent shifts caused by a change in the observer’s viewpoint. Parallax helps estimate distances.
What this mission can help us understand
Its catalogs can provide a reference for interpreting stars on the map. These are position and motion measurements, rather than a sequence of infrared images like SPHEREx observations.
Official source · MAST
WISE / NEOWISENASA · historical observationsReveals the infrared sky and records its changes.
What it does
WISE mapped the sky in infrared. Its later phase, NEOWISE, resumed using the telescope with a focus on studying asteroids and comets.
What it observes
Produced infrared images and brightness measurements on different dates. Data collection ended in 2024: the available records are historical, with no live feed.
What this mission can help us understand
Comparing the records helps study changes in brightness and track moving objects, such as asteroids and comets.
Official source · NASA
NEOSSatCanadian Space Agency (CSA)Observes small objects and tracks their motion.
What it does
The Canadian telescope observes asteroids and comets. It also tracks satellites and space debris: fragments of objects in orbit.
What it observes
Records images to track objects in selected areas of the sky and around Earth. It is not an all-sky infrared survey.
What this mission can help us understand
Its observations help study the paths of small bodies and the behavior of satellites and debris, supporting safety in space.
Official source · CSA
